## Presence without the filter

For many overseas technology decision-makers, investors, and industrialists working across borders, China's industrial ecology stays behind a blur of information. The real depth of Chinese industry does not live in headlines, and a few fundraising slides will not show it. It lives in the machine noise of a Shenzhen workshop, in Beijing's research institutes and think tanks, and in the corridors of multinational headquarters in Shanghai. We regularly curate small-circle, high-density field visits for overseas executives, science and technology teams, investors, and second-generation industrial families in China:<br>
**Beijing**: walk into leading research institutes and offshore-enclave systems, and read the logic of how national hard-tech results become products.<br>
**Shenzhen**: go into precision die work, injection molding and assembly, and rapid prototyping shops, and feel the Greater Bay Area’s twenty-four-hour hardware loop on the floor.<br>
**Shanghai**: connect with multinational engineering R&D hubs and with industry–academia networks in Japan, Southeast Asia, Latin America, the Middle East, and Europe, for closed-door talks among decision-makers.<br>

## What we visit

**Ecosystem hubs and innovation infrastructure**: not only frontier software and industrial-internet parks, but also high-density incubation communities built around “one person + AI” (including the Zhongguancun AI North-Latitude community, Dagongfang’s hardware innovation hub, and Shanghai’s Modelspeed large-model ecology). The point is to see, on site, how a younger generation of Chinese makers actually draws on dense compute and industrial support.<br>
**Hard tech and cross-border collaboration at the frontier**: advanced manufacturing, embodied intelligence, and robotics on the line. Visits include national platforms such as the Zhongguancun Humanoid Robot Innovation Center and joint university labs, plus conversations with multinational engineering R&D hubs and industry-society experts from Europe, the US, and Japan — close enough to feel the tempo as intelligent hardware moves from lab POC to the factory floor.<br>
**Agile hardware and supply-chain manufacturing clusters**: precision injection dies in the Greater Bay Area, SMT, and twenty-four-hour rapid-prototype shops. This is not a showroom walk. It is small-batch, thousand-piece flexible trial production and assembly, so the bottom of the world’s most agile hardware supply chain can be seen as it actually runs.<br>
There are no surface lectures and no assembly-line group photos. What we offer is practical closed-door talk — among decision-makers, and between decision-makers and people on the line. Being physically there is how lasting trust is built among cross-border technology, capacity, and commercial capital.
